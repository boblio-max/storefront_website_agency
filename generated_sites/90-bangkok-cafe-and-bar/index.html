Six reasons the wok stays hot

Every dish from the 90° Bangkok cafe and bar kitchen is spice-scored 1 to 3, so you can order bold without playing roulette. Wok times are honest; leftovers are rare.

Why 90° Bangkok

Bangkok, unfiltered

The real Bangkok isn't a slogan — it's a street stall at midnight, a shrimp broth that clears your sinuses, and a cocktail made with a heavy hand. That's the whole idea behind 90° Bangkok cafe and bar.

Bangkok sits near the 13° north latitude line — practically the equator of flavor. The 90° in our name is a tilt: a nod to the city's heat, and a deliberate turn away from the pale, timid Thai you find in strip-mall food courts. We opened 90° Bangkok cafe and bar in Bothell so you don't have to fly fourteen hours to eat like that.

  • Recipes carried back from Bangkok street stalls — tamarind and palm sugar by the ladle, never a powder mix.
  • Every dish spice-scored 1 to 3 on the card, so you know exactly what "bold" costs you.
  • A kitchen and a bar under one roof at 1427 228th St SE — lunch, dinner and late pours.
  • Pacific Northwest produce meeting Bangkok technique — that's the 90° Bangkok point of view.
