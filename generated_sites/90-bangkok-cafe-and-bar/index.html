01 The board

Six things to eat
the first time

The wok runs on charcoal, so nothing here waits. Prices are in dollars, the way the board reads at the pass. Ask for the mala meter if you want the heat dialled.

01

Pad Kee Mao

$19

Rice noodles tossed in a wok with holy basil, garlic, Thai bird’s eye chili and split river prawns. Drunk, not soupy — order it the way the kitchen eats it.

2 of 3 heat · serves 1 · 12 min

02

Gaeng Gaew

$21

Northern Thai red curry, sticky with roasted chili paste, charred kabob chicken and a stem of holy basil. Northern cooking from Chiang Mai, not Bangkok soft.

3 of 3 heat · serves 1–2 · 14 min

03

Phat Thai

$17

The Bangkok original: rice noodles, egg, tamarind, bean sprouts, chive and a snowfall of crushed peanut. Add a fried egg, or a whole tiger prawn, for two dollars more.

1 of 3 heat · serves 1 · 9 min

04

Tom Kha Nueva

$16

Coconut broth with galangal, lemongrass, kaffir lime leaf and oyster mushroom, holding eight hours of braised short rib. Winter only, until the coconut runs out.

1 of 3 heat · serves 2 · 10 min

05

Khao Niao Mamuang

$11

Mango sticky rice, toasted mung bean, coconut cream poured at the table. Ripened Ataulfo mango, flown in Thursday — when there is any, which is most weeks.

no heat · serves 2 · 5 min

06

Bird’s Eye Margarita

$15

House tequila, ancho and bird’s eye chili infusion, lime and a salt rim, served in a chilled coupe. The infusion is steeped five days in the back room and poured until it runs out — usually around midnight.

2 of 3 heat · house cocktail · 4 min

Full board, gluten-free noodle swap and the kids’ menu live at the pass — call (425) 481-6800 and we’ll read it to you.

02 Our kitchen

The ninety‑degree turn

In 2013 the space at 1427 228th St SE was an auto‑parts bay with bad light and a roll‑up door too heavy to argue with. Mai and Tou pulled the racks out, set a charcoal wok under the old hood, and cooked the first Gaew skewers for a Bothell crowd that had no idea northern Thai food was a two‑hour trip away.

The name is the driveway. You come up 228th, you take a hard ninety at the door, and the whole room changes. Thirteen years later the wok is still charcoal, the chili paste is still pounded to order, and the same corner of Bothell still pulls a queue on a Friday by nine.

Mornings are the cafe half — pour‑overs, Khao Niao Mamuang, a soft‑boiled egg on Lao coffee. Evenings are the bar half. The kitchen closes at ten; the room does not.

  • Charcoal wok, no gas range in the main line
  • Chili paste pounded daily, bird’s eye sourced weekly
  • Gluten‑free rice noodles and a full vegan board
  • Private room for six, walk‑ins welcome at the bar
